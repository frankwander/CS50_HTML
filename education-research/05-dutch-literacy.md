# Dutch literacy — Songbirds equivalents

Context: Dutch is the home language. Bas learns English phonics at school from age 4.
(For reference, the English series is **Julia Donaldson's Songbirds Phonics**, Oxford Reading Tree.)

## Why there's no exact equivalent

**Dutch has a transparent orthography.** Songbirds needs six elaborate phonics phases because
English spelling is chaotic. Dutch letters map to sounds reliably, so Dutch children typically
crack decoding within months. The system is built differently — one core school method plus a
national levelling scale.

## The closest match in spirit: **"ik lees"** (Zwijsen)

The real Songbirds analogue — well-known Dutch children's authors commissioned to write good
stories at controlled reading levels.
- Authors include **Anna Woltz**, Annemarie van den Brink, Elisa van Spronsen
- Starts at **AVI Start**, then M3, E3, M4, E4
- Full colour, larger format, made to feel like "a real book"
- First AVI Start titles: *Niet doen, dot!*, *Pas op voor de bek!*, *Oet gaat op zoek*,
  *Juf in de boom*, *Heel veel haar*, *Kijk uit voor muis!*

Also **"Ik leer lezen met Zwijsen"** — sold as a 6-title AVI Start pack. Easiest first purchase.

## The core scheme: **Veilig leren lezen** (kim-versie)

Used by **over three-quarters of Dutch primary schools**. The structural equivalent of a phonics
programme — decodable booklets matched exactly to letters taught so far.

- **maan, roos, vis** (moon, rose, fish) are the iconic Dutch first-reader words
- Three differentiation lines: **maan** (base) / **zon** (ahead) / **ster** (extra support)
- Zwijsen runs parent-facing **"Mijn kind leert lezen!"** pages per kern

### ⚠️ All kernen belong to ONE year: groep 3 (NL) = 1e leerjaar (BE), age 6–7

The kernen are periods *within* that year, not separate years.

- **Kern start**: 2 weeks · **Kern 1–6**: 2 weeks each · **Kern 7–11**: 3 weeks each
- kim-versie = kern start + 1–11 + **kern afsluiting**. **"Kern 12" is the older 2e maanversie** —
  watch for this when buying second-hand.

**Kern ≠ AVI level.** Kern says which letters are covered; AVI says how hard a text is. The
**zon** line is already at **AVI M3/E3 in kern 1–3**.

## AVI — the thing to shop by

The Dutch book-band system: **AVI Start → M3 → E3 → M4 → E4…**
("M" = midden/middle of year, "E" = eind/end; the number is the groep.) Nearly every Dutch
children's book is marked with one.

## Other series worth knowing

- **Bolleboos** (Zwijsen) — built for children reading **far ahead of their age**: longer
  stories, higher reading level, age-appropriate content. Likely relevant within a couple of years.
- **Kik en Joep** — comic strips spanning AVI Start–M3–E3
- **Mijn zelfleesboek voor groep 3** — grows AVI Start → M3 → E3, with popular characters
  (Dolfje Weerwolfje, Spekkie en Sproet, Agent en Boef)
- **Zoeklicht** (Zwijsen) — struggling readers 10–12, dyslexia-friendly. Not relevant now.

## Alternative method: **Lijn 3** (Malmberg)

First Dutch method built from **the letter** rather than the whole word — closer to English
synthetic phonics in philosophy. Growing in schools. Worth knowing if Veilig leren lezen feels
too whole-word.

## Activity books & games (what Bas actually likes)

### Now, at 4
- **Deltas "Oefenboek met stickers — Ik leer al schrijven (4–5 jaar)"** — trace, then write,
  then place a sticker. **~100 stickers.** Start here.
- Deltas **Ik leer al tellen (4–5 jaar)**; **Mijn eerste ABC oefenboek** (Montessori, stickers)
- **Mijn Letterboekje** (Heutink voor thuis) — all letters and letter combinations, practised
  **visually and auditorily**. One of their most-ordered items for reading-ready preschoolers.
- **Magnetische Letterdoos** — magnetic letters, explicitly from age 4

### Games
- **Letterbingo Veilig leren lezen** (Zwijsen) — 2×48 bingo cards; goal is linking sounds to letters
- **Letterkwartet** — quartet card game for distinguishing sounds and matching to letters
- *(Both labelled 6+ but the sound-letter play works earlier with a parent — and phonemic
  awareness is the strongest predictor of later reading.)*

### Digital
- **Squla Junior** — app for **ages 3–6**; letters, numbers, shapes. No advertising, no chat.
  Full Squla covers groep 1–8, 70,000+ quiz questions, parent dashboard. Standard choice for
  Dutch families abroad.

### Later, when decoding starts (~5–6)
- **Veilig & Vlot** booklets — one per kern; word rows increasing in difficulty, for **fluency**
- **Werkboekjes** from kern 1 — technical reading, comprehension, spelling; **maan/zon** versions

## Timing — the bilingual twist

In NL, formal reading starts at **groep 3, age 6**. Ages 4–5 (groep 1–2) are play-based
*voorbereidend lezen* — **klankbewustzijn** (phonemic awareness) and letter knowledge.

But Bas starts English phonics at four, so he'll decode before Dutch peers begin. Because Dutch
is the more transparent language, decoding **transfers into Dutch easily**.

**Suggested sequence:** build spoken Dutch vocabulary and sound-awareness now (reading aloud —
Nijntje/Dick Bruna, *Jip en Janneke*, Kikker/Max Velthuijs; rhyming and sound games). Introduce
**ik lees** at AVI Start once English decoding is secure, ~5–6. Expect him to move up AVI levels
fast — possibly entering on the **zon** line or skipping kernen and choosing straight by AVI.
