# STEM progression — workbook and project series

A multi-year sequence rather than a shopping list, aligned with the **depth-not-acceleration**
finding in `03-evidence-base.md`.

## The headline recommendation: **Beast Academy**

[Beast Academy](https://artofproblemsolving.com/store/list/beast-academy) (Art of Problem
Solving) — comic-book-style maths curriculum **explicitly designed for mathematically gifted
children who find the normal curriculum too easy**. Reviewers call it "the most challenging math
program available for these grade levels."

- **Levels 1–5**, each split into four books (A–D)
- **Guide Book** (graphic-novel instruction, non-consumable) + **Practice Book**
  (300–400 problems and puzzles with full solutions)
- Covers grade-level standards **in far greater depth** rather than racing ahead;
  levels 4–5 reach middle-school content
- Roughly **ages 6–13** — carries him from Year 2 to the 11+

**Why it fits strategically:** it's **depth, not acceleration** — exactly what the evidence
supports *and* what his school's mastery approach will endorse. It won't put you at odds with
his teachers.

*Caveat:* American, so "grades" not "years" (grade 2 ≈ Year 3). UK retail availability not
confirmed — the AoPS store ships from the US; there's also an online version.

## Now — ages 4–6 (Reception–Y1)

- **Numberblocks** (CBeebies) — free, genuinely excellent. Numbers as cube arrangements;
  addition as combining, subtraction as splitting. Widely used by Reception teachers.
- **Numicon and Cuisenaire rods** — the **EEF's early-maths guidance names both specifically**
  as powerful tools for building number sense. Cheap, tactile, last for years.
- **KiwiCo Koala Crate** (2–4) → **Kiwi Crate** (5–8) — monthly project boxes, ships to the UK
  from ~$5.95, pausable any time. The "activity book he loves" in STEM form.
- **ScratchJr** — free app, ages 5–7, drag-and-drop blocks with big icons and **no reading
  required**, so it works before literacy.

## Ages 6–9 (Years 2–4)

- **Beast Academy Levels 1–3** — the spine
- **Scratch** (browser) — most children move over at 7–8; teaches loops, variables, conditionals,
  events properly
- **Snap Circuits** (8+) — 75+ real electronics experiments
- **LEGO Spike Essential** — roughly Years 1–5
- **KiwiCo Atlas Crate** (6–11) alongside Kiwi Crate

## Ages 9–11 (Years 5–6) — the 11+ window

- **Beast Academy Levels 4–5**
- **CrunchLabs Build Box** (Mark Rober) — ages 8–14, real engineering builds with a video
  walkthrough per box. ~$329.40/yr, plus **£8–15/box UK shipping** and 2–4 weeks' delivery.
- **Tinker Crate** (9–16); **MEL Science** for lab-grade chemistry
- **micro:bit → Python** — programmable in Scratch first, then Python. Children with a solid
  Scratch foundation reportedly move through Python fundamentals **30–40% faster** than those
  starting cold.
- **LEGO Spike Prime**

## The competition ladder

Matters more than it looks — SMPY found the biggest side-benefit of enrichment was introducing
able children to *others like them*.

| Competition | Ages / years | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Maths Challenge** (Mathematical Association) | 9–11 | Light on formal knowledge; solvable by logical reasoning |
| **Primary Kangaroo** (UKMT) | 9–11, Y5/6 or below | Part of the international Kangaroo family |
| **Junior Mathematical Challenge** (UKMT) | Y8 and below | Can enter early. 700,000+ take part in UKMT challenges annually |

## Curriculum workbook spine

For the "keep up with and slightly ahead of school" layer:

- **Maths — No Problem!** — Singapore mastery methodology; best match to how English primaries
  now actually teach
- **White Rose Maths** workbooks (CGP and OUP editions) — White Rose is the scheme many English
  primaries use, so this mirrors his classroom
- **CGP** — study books plus separate workbooks, so the study book is reusable
- **Schofield & Sims** — good value, strong at KS1; some find KS2 light on practice volume

## Suggested sequencing

1. **Now:** Numberblocks + Numicon, with a KiwiCo subscription as the "activity box" habit
2. **~5:** add ScratchJr
3. **6–7:** start **Beast Academy** and let it run five years as the backbone
4. **7–8:** layer in Snap Circuits and Scratch
5. **From 9:** competitions
6. **9–10:** CrunchLabs and micro:bit as the 11+ push begins
