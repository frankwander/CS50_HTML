# The 11+ strategy

## The honest starting point

At four, the lever is **not** exam prep. Nothing bought now is 11+ preparation. The children
who win the hardest places seven years out are, at this age, fluent readers with strong number
sense, real curiosity, and the confidence to talk to an adult. Selective schools actively screen
*against* the over-coached.

What to put in place now is not tutoring but an **oversight system**: a professional who
benchmarks him yearly and tells you honestly whether he's being stretched. That is the thing a
state school will not do for you, and the thing private-school fees really buy.

## The target: two tracks reachable from Peckham

### Track A — top London independents (fee-paying, ~£24–30k/yr)

Dulwich College, Alleyn's (both on the Dulwich estate, minutes away), City of London,
Highgate, KCS Wimbledon, St Paul's, UCS, Latymer Upper, Emanuel, Whitgift, Trinity.

Shared machinery means one prep track serves several schools:
- **ISEB Common Pre-Test**
- The South London **"Quest"** / London consortium format

Fees remove the geography problem entirely; competition is on merit and interview.

### Track B — the Sutton super-selective grammars (free, state)

Wilson's, Sutton Grammar, Wallington County. Among the best schools in England — Wilson's
routinely tops national state league tables. Entry via the **Sutton SET consortium exam**.

**The catch is catchment, and it's serious:**
- **Sutton Grammar has a catchment** (SM1–7, KT4, KT17, CR0 4, CR4). SE15 falls outside it.
- **Wilson's has no catchment** — ranks on score, then straight-line distance. That makes it
  the one realistic Sutton target from Peckham, but the distance tie-break is brutal
  out-of-borough.
- ~3,282 boys sat the SET for a few hundred places in a recent cycle.

**Verdict:** treat Sutton as a high-value long-shot, not the spine of the plan — unless you
would consider moving closer. The independents are the realistic core.

## Roadmap: age 4 → 11

Bas sits the 11+ in **autumn 2032** for **September 2033** entry.

| Age | Year | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 4–6 | Reception–Y1 | Foundations. Daily reading for pleasure, number sense, broad play-based enrichment. **Commission one expert baseline assessment.** No exam prep. |
| 7–8 | Y2–Y3 | Start the slow-maturing strands — instrument, chess, a sport. Deepen maths. Keep the annual assessment as early warning. |
| 9 | Y4 | Introduce reasoning gently (as puzzles). **Lock the target school list** with a consultant. First formal 11+ diagnostic. |
| 10 | Y5 | Structured prep begins in earnest — format-specific tutoring, mocks, interview practice. |
| 11 | Y6 | Exam year. Register and sit ISEB/consortium papers, Dulwich & Alleyn's exams, Sutton SET if in play. Interviews in January. |

## The annual operating model

One expert assessment a year + a termly guidance touchpoint + one or two live enrichment
strands + camps in the holidays.

| Tier | Cost/yr | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| Light | £2–4k | Baseline assessment every 2–3 yrs, one paid strand + free clubs, one camp per break |
| Medium | £6–10k | Annual benchmarking, music + STEM + sport year-round, camps, light consultant check-in |
| Consultant-managed | £15k+ | Retained consultant/mentor, annual psychometric + termly appraisals, full enrichment, tutoring from Y5 |

For scale: the private fee declined is **~£30,000 a year, every year**. Even the top tier here
spends roughly half that.

## Honest caveats

1. **Don't buy tutoring at four.** Wasted money, and real risk of dulling the curiosity
   selective schools are looking for.
2. **The Sutton catchment is the quiet dealbreaker.** From SE15 the honest lever for a free
   grammar place is moving closer.
3. **The annual assessment is the purchase that pays.** It directly answers "is he being
   stretched?" and is comparatively cheap. Prioritise it.
4. **Watch for over-scheduling.** A tired, over-managed child interviews worse than a happy
   curious one. Two or three sustained strands beat a packed timetable.
5. **Interview two or three consultants before retaining one.** Choose the person you'd trust
   to tell you an uncomfortable truth about your son.
6. **His STEM interest is an asset.** A child with genuine passion is what these schools select
   for. Feed it rather than broadening for its own sake.
